Rail Siege

A single-player survivors game for the browser, in the style of Transport Tycoon. You build a rail network with the mouse. Your trains carry cargo to the town and shoot the horde on the way. Keep the town alive for 15 minutes and beat the boss.

Genre
Survivors + tycoon

Decisions to approve

This document uses the defaults below. Say OK to approve them all, or name the number to change.

  1. Building: click a station, then a second station. The route finder picks the tiles. Waypoints are optional. Time runs at 25 % while you build.
  2. Stations: fixed, one per industry, placed by the map seed. Your own stations come later.
  3. Trains: one train per line, push-pull shuttle. Trains do not block or crash. No signals in the MVP.
  4. Weapons: company technology. Every train carries every weapon wagon you own.
  5. XP: from delivered cargo, not from kills.
  6. Run: 15 minutes, then the Large UFO. You lose when the town reaches 0 HP.
  7. Map: 48 × 32 tiles, top-down, camera pan and 2 zoom levels.
  8. Code: keep the stack and about 60 % of the code. Rebuild on main in 10 commits.

1

Core loop

You are the railway company. You do not drive. You plan lines with the mouse, and your trains run them on their own. Each train carries cargo to the town and shoots the horde on the way. Delivered cargo gives money and XP. Money builds more lines. XP gives cards.

Win and lose

The town has 100 HP. Enemies that reach the town hit it. At 0 HP the run ends: "The town fell". At 15:00 the Large UFO comes. Kill it to win.

One run

One map of 48 × 32 tiles, random seed per run. 15 minutes and a boss. No meta progression in the MVP. Expected: 25 to 35 cards per winning run.

2

Map and stations

The town is in the centre. Six industries stand around it. Each industry has one station tile. The rule is simple: far means rich. Far industries make more cargo and pay more per tile, but they are near the map edge, where the horde spawns.

IndustryCargoDistance to townOutputPay per unit per tilePer map
Coal mineCoal8 to 12 tiles1.0 unit/s$0.122
ForestWood10 to 14 tiles1.2 unit/s$0.101
FarmGrain8 to 14 tiles1.0 unit/s$0.101
Iron mineIron ore14 to 18 tiles1.5 unit/s$0.141
Oil wellsOil18 to 22 tiles2.0 unit/s$0.181

An industry keeps its output in a stock (maximum 40 units). Trains take from the stock. Two trains on one industry share its output, so more trains do not always mean more money.

Overrun: when 6 or more enemies stand within 2 tiles of a station, the industry stops and its station flashes red. Clear the enemies to restart it.

Building lines

This replaces the track laying of the MVP. You never lay track tile by tile, and you never build under full time pressure.

Track uses the tile model of the MVP: 6 piece bits per tile, like OpenTTD TrackBits. The route becomes a list of pieces with curves, so trains follow the rails exactly.

Money and XP

pay = units × rate × route length XP = units card = when XP reaches 15 + 10 × level

Example: a coal mine 10 tiles from the town. A full train carries 20 units: 20 × $0.12 × 10 = $24 per trip. The mine makes only 1 unit/s, so the line earns about $1.20/s, or $72 per minute. Oil wells 20 tiles away earn 2 × $0.18 × 20 = $7.20/s, six times more, at the edge of the map.

Start: $100 and one free line from the town to the nearest coal mine, with an armoured wagon at level 1.

The horde

Enemies spawn in groups at the map edge and walk over grass and water. They do not need track. Each type has its own target, so the horde attacks the whole company, not only one point.

Broken track: a train that reaches a broken tile stops and reverses, so its line gets shorter and pays less. Click the tile to repair it, or take the Repair crew card.

6

Cards

When the XP bar is full, the game pauses and shows 3 random cards. Weapons are company technology: every train carries every weapon wagon you own. So each new line adds firepower, and each weapon level makes all trains stronger. Every item has 5 levels. When all items are at level 5, the card is "Subsidy: +$150".

Coal hoppers connect weapons and network: every line becomes a strip of fire. A dense network near the town with hoppers is a strong defence. This synergy only a railway game can have.

Technical design

The stack does not change: Rust, macroquad 0.4.16, WebAssembly, mise. All rules stay in the pure sim crate with unit tests. About 60 % of the current code stays.

ModuleStatusChange
math.rs, rng.rs, grid.rskeepNo change.
track.rschangeKeep tile map, piece bits and piece geometry. Add stations, industries and a broken flag. Map 48 × 32.
route.rsnewA* over tiles with waypoints and cost. Turns the tile path into pieces.
train.rschangeKeep car placement on pieces and curves. Remove steering, auto build and self-hit. Add route follow, push-pull, load and unload.
enemy.rschangeKeep movement, separation, bus and UFO logic. Add saboteur. Targets become town, track or train.
director.rschangeSpawn at the map edge, not in a ring. New cap curve and timeline.
weapon.rskeepSame three wagons. The world loops over all trains.
cards.rschangeSame roll logic. New pool: company and town cards.
world.rschangeLarge rewrite: lines, trains, money, industries, town HP, build commands.
Crates and pickup radiusremoveXP comes from delivered cargo now.
game/render.rschangeKeep map, track, train and enemy drawing. Add camera pan and zoom, stations, ghost route.
game/ui.rschangeKeep card screen and screens. New top bar and train panel.
mise.toml, web/, workspacekeepSame tasks: run, test, lint, serve, ci.

                      cargo: u32, hp: f32, wreck: f32, cars: Vec<Car> }
pub enum Command    { Build { a, b, via }, Repair(Tile), Remove(LineId), Pick(usize) }

pub struct World {
    map: TileMap, stations: Vec<Station>, lines: Vec<Line>, trains: Vec<Train>,
    enemies: Vec<Enemy>, wagons: Vec<Wagon>, shots: Vec<Shot>, fires: Fires,
    money: u32, xp: u32, level: u32, town_hp: f32, time: f32, state: State, rng: Pcg32,
}
impl World { pub fn step(&mut self, cmds: &[Command]) { /* order below */ } }

One simulation step (60 per second)

  1. Apply commands: build, repair, remove, pick card.
  2. Industries produce into their stock. Check overrun.
  3. Move trains. Load, unload, pay money and XP. Place cars on the pieces.
  4. Spawn director: add groups at the map edge up to the cap.
  5. Move enemies to their targets. Saboteurs break track.
  6. Fire weapons. Move shots. Tick coal fires.
  7. Resolve hits, ram, touch damage, wrecks and town damage.
  8. If the XP bar is full, change the state to LevelUp and roll 3 cards.

Slow time lives in the game crate: during a build it runs the fixed step 4 times less often. The sim stays deterministic.

Tests in sim

  • Route: avoids water, prefers existing track, honours waypoints, returns None when blocked, cost counts only new tiles.
  • Train: car positions on a route with curves, push-pull reverse, load and unload amounts, stop at broken track.
  • Economy: pay formula, shared stock between two trains, XP thresholds.
  • Same seed and same commands give the same run.

MVP scope and commit plan

In the MVP

  • 48 × 32 map with lakes, town and 6 industries.
  • Build tool with route finder, waypoints, slow time, cost.
  • Trains with push-pull, cargo, money and XP.
  • Repair and remove lines.
  • Residents, saboteurs, buses, small UFO, Large UFO.
  • 3 weapon wagons, 8 company and town cards.
  • Top bar, train panel, all screens.

After the MVP

  • Signals and train blocking.
  • Supply chains (iron ore to steel mill to town).
  • Place your own stations. Bridges and tunnels.
  • Isometric OpenTTD view, sound.
  • Era evolutions: steam to diesel to electric.
  • GitHub Pages deploy.

The build thread commits on main, one logical step per commit, with Conventional Commits. The build tool is playable after commit 4. This lets us test the main risk first: does building feel good now?
