Transport Survivors: new concepts

Four new ways to make a survivors game with OpenTTD trains. All four drop the track laying while driving. Three of them move toward Transport Tycoon: build a network, earn money, run many trains. One concept is recommended, and you can play a sketch of it in this page.

What went wrong in the MVP

Dennis played the MVP and gave two points: the track laying was bad, and the game must feel more like Transport Tycoon. The figure shows the cause. In the MVP you lay track while you drive. You must make one choice per tile, under fire. In Transport Tycoon you plan a line once, and the trains drive it many times.

A

Rail Siege

recommended

Build a rail network with the mouse. Your trains carry cargo to the town and shoot the horde on the way. Money buys more trains. The network is your weapon.

You control
Mouse: build, buy, upgrade
Lose
Town HP is 0
Run
15 min + boss

How it plays

  • Click a station, then click a second station. The game finds the shortest route (like the OpenTTD autorail tool) and shows the cost. Click again to build.
  • Time runs at 25 % while you build. Space pauses. You never lay track under time pressure.
  • Each line gets a train. Trains shuttle and follow OpenTTD rules: acceleration, signals, slower when long.
  • Cargo pays only when it arrives in the town. Rich industries are far away, in horde land.
  • Wagons shoot automatically. Locomotives ram. A busy line is a moving wall.
  • Money fills a bar. Each full bar pauses the game and shows 3 company cards: new train, weapon wagon, signal, faster engine, repair crew.

Try it: playable sketch

Money $0 Town HP 20 Trains 1 Time 0:00 Speed ×1

Click a station, then click a second station to build a line.

A new line costs $5 per new tile plus $40 for the train. Only lines to the town pay.

Why it is fun

  • Closest to Transport Tycoon: build, earn, expand.
  • Track building is calm and planned. This fixes the MVP problem.
  • Greed against safety: the long line to the rich coal mine runs through the horde.
  • Survivors parts stay: horde, auto fire, cards, short run.

Problems

  • No hero on screen. It can feel like tower defense.
  • Pathfinding for many trains and signals is new work.
Code reuse
60 %

Keeps track bits, trails, enemies, director, weapons, cards. Removes steering. Adds mouse build tool, routes, money.

B

Last Town

One walled town in the middle of the map. The town fights, but only with what your trains bring in. Coal powers the guns. Steel repairs the walls. You build the supply lines out into the horde.

You control
Mouse: build, repair
Lose
Town walls fall
Run
20 min + siege

How it plays

  • The map has industries like OpenTTD: coal mine, iron mine, steel mill, farm.
  • Supply chains matter. Iron ore goes to the steel mill. Steel goes to the town.
  • The horde attacks trains and breaks track. You send a repair crew or build a bypass.
  • Tower fire rate depends on the coal stock. An empty stock stops the guns.

Why it is fun

  • Real OpenTTD supply chains with high stakes.
  • Each lost line hurts. Each repaired line feels like a win.

Problems

  • Feedback is slow and indirect. The fun is in the planning, less in the action.
  • Closer to tower defense than to survivors.
Code reuse
45 %

Keeps track bits, trails, enemies, director. Weapons move from wagons to towers. Needs industries and stockpiles.

C

Station Stop

You drive one train on your own network, like in the MVP, but you never lay track while you drive. At each station the game pauses, and you build new track pieces with the mouse.

You control
Keys: drive. Mouse: build
Lose
Train HP is 0
Run
10 min + boss

How it plays

  • W and S set the throttle. A and D pick the branch at the next junction.
  • Momentum like OpenTTD: a long train needs time to speed up and to stop.
  • Station stops come every 60 to 90 s. Delivered cargo gives money for track pieces and wagons.

Why it is fun

  • Direct action. You are the train.
  • Highest code reuse. Fastest to test.

Problems

  • Still a driving game. It is close to the MVP that did not work.
  • Two modes (drive and build) break the flow.
Code reuse
75 %

Keeps nearly all of sim. Replaces auto build with a build break and a throttle.

D

Signal Box

A full rail network is on the map from the start. Many armed trains run on it. You are the dispatcher. You click points and signals to send trains to the horde and to keep them from crashing.

You control
Mouse: points, signals
Lose
3 stations lost
Run
12 min + boss

How it plays

  • No building. The network is generated for each run, with loops, junctions and stations.
  • Two trains on one block crash. Signals stop that, but block the line.
  • More trains means more firepower and more crash risk.

Why it is fun

  • A real-time railway puzzle. Signals are the OpenTTD skill everyone remembers.
  • No track building at all.

Problems

  • No network growth, so less tycoon.
  • Crashes can feel unfair. Fast clicks on a busy map are stressful.
Code reuse
65 %

Keeps track bits, trails, enemies, weapons. Adds network generator, signals and block logic.

Reuse is the share of the current 3,700 lines of Rust that stays. The mise tasks, the macroquad setup and the web build stay in all four concepts.

Recommendation: A, Rail Siege

Rail Siege is the most Transport Tycoon of the four. You build a network, earn money and grow. It keeps the survivors parts that work: a growing horde, automatic weapons, 1 of 3 cards, a short run. It removes the problem: you plan track with the mouse, at slow time, and the game finds the route.

Ideas from the other concepts can come later as cards: signals from D, supply chains from B.
